Milestone 1 · Accelerated ageing

isothermal 25 °C · 40 cycles SEI layer growth plus irreversible lithium plating. Protocols ranked by how much capacity is left after each cycle.

MCC 2C-1C is the clear sweet spot. 6.6 minutes faster than plain 1C, and only 1.0 percentage point of capacity lost. Nothing else comes close on the Pareto frontier.

Lithium plating is the main problem, not SEI growth. And it gets worse fast with higher C-rates. Plain CC-CV 2C loses 0.555 Ah to plating, but MCC 2C-1C drops that to 0.450 Ah by keeping the first stage moderate.

Milestone 1 · Thermal baseline

lumped model · h = 10 W m⁻² K⁻¹ Degradation switched off — just heat from I²R losses and the entropic voltage term.

CC-CV 3C hits 70.1 °C at h = 10. That's way above the 45 °C safety threshold. Actually everything faster than 1C crosses 45 °C — active cooling is mandatory, not optional.

Interesting: temperature peaks before charge ends, during the CC phase. Once we switch to CV, current drops, heating stops, and the cell starts cooling down while we're still on the tail.

Milestone 2 · Cooling sensitivity

h = 1 / 10 / 100 Same 7 protocols, three different cooling levels — see how much thermal management actually matters.

At h = 100 (good liquid cooling) every single protocol stays under 45 °C. Even the hottest one, MCC 4C-3C-2C, only reaches 44.4 °C. But at h = 1 — basically natural convection — even CC-CV 1C hits 48.2 °C and CC-CV 3C goes up to 110.5 °C. Bottom line: thermal management matters more than protocol design for safety.

Milestone 3 · Real-world calibration

NASA + CALCE datasets The model's degradation is accelerated on purpose. Here's how 80 simulated cycles translate to real-world battery life.
Scale factor = real cycles ÷ simulated cycles. The accelerated 80-cycle study covers roughly 1000 real 1C cycles or 400 real 2C cycles.
NASA 1C/1C → scale ≈ 13–16×. CALCE 2C/1C → scale ≈ 5× (makes sense — 2C kills the cell faster).

Milestone 4 · 96-cell pack

8 × 12 grid · thermal coupling 8×12 grid of 21700 cells. Centre cells have less airflow so get ~30% less cooling than edges — realistic pack effect.

Good news: the protocol ranking doesn't change between single-cell and pack simulations. Centre cells run about 0.1–0.5 °C above edge cells, but the ordering stays the same. So single-cell results do scale up — no surprises at the pack level here.

With strong active cooling (h = 100) the whole pack sits at 26–34 °C no matter what protocol we use. That's comfortably inside the safe zone — even at pack level, thermal management is the real answer.
